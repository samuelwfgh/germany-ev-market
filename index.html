Used-car market and prices

Germany's used-car market is much larger than the new-car market and changes drivetrain mix more slowly. KBA calls used-car transactions Besitzumschreibungen (ownership transfers). KBA FZ 9 reports these monthly and includes passenger-car fuel / drivetrain characteristics. Price figures below are actual average prices paid by private buyers in the DAT survey — not list prices.

Why this matters: electrification reaches the used market with a delay. In July 2026 BEVs were 4.5% of used-car transfers, while BEVs were 29.3% of new registrations in the same month. The difference is a useful measure of how slowly the transition in new-car demand propagates through the installed fleet and second-hand market.
Used-market methodology: I do not fabricate an August drivetrain split. August's total transfer volume is already available, but the detailed monthly fuel mix used for this chart currently runs through July. Where KBA secondary publications provide rounded category shares rather than the full count table, the chart uses those published shares plus exact BEV/PHEV counts and assigns only the rounding remainder to “Other”.

Refuelling and charging infrastructure

Germany's energy infrastructure is changing at very different speeds. Conventional street filling stations have been broadly stable around 14,000 sites, public EV charging points have expanded rapidly, and the public hydrogen network has recently contracted and shifted toward higher-throughput commercial-vehicle sites.

Hydrogen count corrected: the previous 113 figure has been removed. It came from a broader historical station database and was not equivalent to “publicly accessible and operational today.” H2 MOBILITY itself described its network as around 70 public stations in September 2024 and then announced several closure waves. Germany's June 2026 IPHE update reports 58 stations in operation as of April 2026; 52 offered 700 bar and 48 offered 350 bar (the pressure-level counts overlap because one station can offer both). Real-time availability can be lower on any given day, so the live H2-Stations map remains the right source for “can I refuel right now?” The site deliberately does not extrapolate an unverified September 2026 stock count.
Do not compare the headline counts one-for-one: a conventional filling station is a site, while the Bundesnetzagentur figure counts individual public charging points. A charging hub can therefore contribute many points. The charts are kept separate to avoid implying false infrastructure equivalence.

Distance travelled

KBA's “Verkehr in Kilometern” series uses odometer differences from periodic technical inspections and modelling. In 2025, German passenger cars travelled an estimated 562.3 billion km in total.

Subsidies, taxes and selected EV policy

Germany's support has changed more abruptly than Norway's. The timeline below uses federal-government sources and focuses on measures that materially affected private EV demand.

2016

The federal Umweltbonus purchase incentive begins. Over its lifetime, roughly €10bn was paid for around 2.1m electric vehicles.

2020

The temporary Innovationsprämie doubles the federal share of the purchase incentive. A qualifying BEV below €40,000 net list price could receive up to €9,000 combined government/manufacturer support.

2023

Direct purchase support is narrowed to BEVs/fuel-cell vehicles at the start of the year. The Umweltbonus then ends abruptly after 17 December 2023.

2026

A new income-targeted programme applies from 1 January 2026: eligible private buyers/leasers of new BEVs, PHEVs or range-extender vehicles can receive €1,500–€6,000 depending on drivetrain, taxable household income and children. The application portal opened 20 May 2026.

2026

Pure EVs first registered through 31 December 2030 can qualify for German motor-vehicle-tax exemption, with the exemption capped at 31 December 2035.

In 2024, about 97.5% of German transport final energy came from fuels and 2.5% from electricity. UBA reports the fuel-energy mix as roughly 49.4% diesel, 29.5% petrol, 16.0% aviation fuels, 5.0% biofuels and 0.4% LPG/natural gas.

Charging-electricity caveat: the UBA series is excellent for a consistent official trend, but it is not a complete meter of all electricity consumed by German EVs. Public charging is based on certified metered quantities; non-public charging is a standardized estimate for BEVs submitted to the THG-quota system. In 2025, UBA says about 58% of eligible vehicles received such a certificate. That is why I do not turn the annual figures into fictional monthly “sales”.
