Distance travelled

KBA's “Verkehr in Kilometern” series uses odometer differences from periodic technical inspections and modelling. In 2025, German passenger cars travelled an estimated 562.3 billion km in total.

Subsidies, taxes and selected EV policy

Germany's support has changed more abruptly than Norway's. The timeline below uses federal-government sources and focuses on measures that materially affected private EV demand.

2016

The federal Umweltbonus purchase incentive begins. Over its lifetime, roughly €10bn was paid for around 2.1m electric vehicles.

2020

The temporary Innovationsprämie doubles the federal share of the purchase incentive. A qualifying BEV below €40,000 net list price could receive up to €9,000 combined government/manufacturer support.

2023

Direct purchase support is narrowed to BEVs/fuel-cell vehicles at the start of the year. The Umweltbonus then ends abruptly after 17 December 2023.

2026

A new income-targeted programme applies from 1 January 2026: eligible private buyers/leasers of new BEVs, PHEVs or range-extender vehicles can receive €1,500–€6,000 depending on drivetrain, taxable household income and children. The application portal opened 20 May 2026.

2026

Pure EVs first registered through 31 December 2030 can qualify for German motor-vehicle-tax exemption, with the exemption capped at 31 December 2035.

Transport energy consumption

The Norway page contains detailed road-energy and biofuel series. For Germany, UBA/TREMOD provides broad transport-energy data and road-fuel series. The charts below use the latest clearly comparable headline values rather than combining incompatible datasets.

In 2024, about 97.5% of German transport final energy came from fuels and 2.5% from electricity. UBA reports the fuel-energy mix as roughly 49.4% diesel, 29.5% petrol, 16.0% aviation fuels, 5.0% biofuels and 0.4% LPG/natural gas.

Useful direct analogue: UBA also publishes downloadable spreadsheet series for road-fuel consumption and passenger-car fuel consumption. Those are linked below so the page can be extended later with the same long-run depth as the original Norway energy section.
